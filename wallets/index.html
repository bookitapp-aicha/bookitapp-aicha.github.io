Wallet & Gift Cards

Every client gets a wallet at each business. Money enters it three ways: a gift card sent to their phone number, a refund the business returns as credit, and the cashback that already exists. The wallet is then spent first at checkout, in the client app or at the front desk. This page is the full plan: the rules, a working demo of both apps with the API calls and database rows behind each tap, the schema, the endpoints, and the order to build it in.

What was decided today

Five answers from 25 Nov settle the gaps in the stories. They change the design more than the stories suggest, mainly because a gift card is now money in the wallet and no longer a separate payment method.

  1. Staff choose where a refund goesWhen staff void a gift card or refund by hand, a popup asks each time.Refund to wallet or to original method of payment?
  2. Bulk cards share one amountA company sale gives every recipient the same amount. One sale, one payment, one card each.
  3. A gift card becomes wallet balanceWhen a card reaches an account, its amount is added to the wallet. The wallet is always spent first.
  4. Wallet credit expiresThe owner sets how long credit lasts. The default is 6 months from the day it is added.
  5. Phone number onlyA gift card is attached to a phone number. Email is never used to match a recipient.
What this plan had to assume on top of those answers is listed under Open questions. The two that matter most: gift cards "do not expire" in story #1090, yet wallet credit now does, and a refund that expires is the client's own money.

How it works

Life of a gift card

A card only exists once it is paid for. From then on it is either waiting for its phone number or sitting in a wallet as credit.

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> pending_payment: Client starts an online purchase
  pending_payment --> [*]: Payment fails and no card is created
  pending_payment --> pending_claim: Paid and the number has no account
  pending_payment --> active: Paid and the number belongs to a client
  [*] --> active: Front desk sale to a client record
  pending_claim --> active: Number verified at sign-up
  pending_claim --> voided: Staff void it and refund in full
  active --> voided: Staff void it and refund what is left
  active --> [*]: Used up or expired

Checkout with the wallet and a card

The wallet part is reserved inside the same database transaction that creates the cart, so two checkouts cannot spend the same credit.

sequenceDiagram
  participant C as Client app
  participant A as API
  participant W as Wallet ledger
  participant G as Paymob
  C->>A: POST /bookings/cart/checkout with use_wallet
  A->>W: Lock the credits and hold EGP 650
  A->>G: Create a session for the remaining EGP 250
  A-->>C: Payment link and the wallet amount applied
  C->>G: Pays by card
  alt Payment succeeds
    G->>A: Webhook success
    A->>W: Hold becomes a posted spend
    A-->>C: Booking confirmed
  else Payment fails or is abandoned
    G->>A: Webhook failure or reconciliation
    A->>W: Hold released and credits restored
    A-->>C: Nothing charged and balance back
  end

Where a refund goes

Applied to each payment behind a booking separately, so a booking paid partly from the wallet and partly by card can split.

flowchart TD
  R[Policy decides the refund amount] --> P{How was this part paid}
  P -->|From the wallet| W1[Back to the wallet under the same gift card]
  P -->|Online or at the venue| Q{Who started the refund}
  Q -->|Staff| POP[Popup asks wallet or original method]
  Q -->|Client cancelled| M{Was it paid online}
  M -->|No| V[Handled as today and the setting does not apply]
  M -->|Yes| T{Refund to wallet setting}
  T -->|On| W2[New wallet credit with an expiry date]
  T -->|Off| O[Paymob refund to the card]
  POP -->|Wallet| W2
  POP -->|Original method| O

Database design

The API already has a WalletLedgers table, created for cashback. It only ever receives credits: nothing debits it, there is no balance endpoint and checkout cannot spend it. This design finishes that ledger instead of starting a second one.

The core idea: every credit row is a lot with its own source, remaining amount and expiry date. A spend is a debit row plus allocation rows saying which lots it drew from. That one structure gives per-credit expiry, soonest-to-expire-first spending, and a full usage history for each gift card after its money has merged into the wallet.

Rules the database enforces

  • Balance is always SUM(remaining_amount) over a client's active, unexpired credits at one business. It is never stored.
  • For every client: credits minus posted and held debits equals the sum of remaining amounts. A nightly check alerts on any mismatch.
  • For every gift card: original = used + expired + voided + remaining.
  • Every spend locks the client's lots in a fixed order with SELECT … FOR UPDATE inside the checkout transaction.
  • Reporting: the custom-reports sandbox reads through ai_reporting views, so the new tables need views there before owners can report on gift cards.

Delivery plan

Six phases. Each one ends with something that can be shown and tested, and each is hidden behind the per-business switch until the last. Sizes are relative, not dates: the team size is not known to this plan.

  1. Ledger foundations

    • Migrations for the config table, the ledger columns and the allocations table. Backfill cashback rows.
    • Wallet service with locking, holds and reversal, and its unit tests.
    • Expiry job and settings endpoints. New capabilities.

    API only · medium · nothing visible yet, but cashback keeps working through the new service.

  2. Selling gift cards

    • Orders and cards tables. Online purchase with webhook. Front desk single and bulk sale.
    • Claim at phone verification. Messages to recipients.
    • Client app gift card form. Business app Gift cards list and assign form.

    All three codebases · large · stories #1090, #1091, #1092. Start WhatsApp template approval here: it has a waiting time outside the team's control.

  3. Seeing the wallet

    • Wallet endpoints for clients and staff.
    • Client app menu item, Wallet screen, front-page chip. Business app profile sections and card detail.

    All three · medium · stories #1094, #1095. After this phase people can receive and see money but not yet spend it, so it should ship together with phase 3.

  4. Spending

    • Cart checkout applies the wallet with holds. Webhook and abandonment release them.
    • Front desk checkout takes a wallet amount.
    • Checkout screens in both apps.

    All three · large · stories #1097, #1098. The riskiest phase: it touches the checkout path every booking uses.

  5. Refunds and voids

    • Refund destination through every refund path. The setting for client cancellations, the popup for staff.
    • Wallet-paid parts return to their gift card. Void a card.
    • Client notice. Wallet configuration screen.

    All three · large · stories #1100, #1102, #1103.

  6. Hardening and launch

    • Concurrency and replay tests against a real database. Ledger balance check job.
    • Reports and Odoo mapping for gift card sales and wallet payments. ai_reporting views.
    • Arabic review. Pilot with one business, then switch on per business.

    Mostly API and QA · medium.

Security and testing

What could go wrong with the money

RiskHow the design stops it
Two checkouts spend the same credit at onceLots are row-locked in a fixed order inside the checkout transaction. The wallet amount is held until payment finishes. A database check keeps remaining_amount at zero or above.
A retried request credits or debits twiceIdempotency keys on gift card orders, voids and refunds, on top of the cart's existing key. Webhook replays do nothing once a transaction has left pending.
Someone claims a card sent to another personA waiting card is only claimed after the WhatsApp code sets phone_verified_at. Typing a number is never enough. The number is already unique among active clients.
Guessing gift card IDsThe ID is a reference, not a voucher code. Nothing can be redeemed by typing it. Money moves only to the account that owns the phone number.
Stolen cards used to buy gift cardsMinimum and maximum amount, a daily limit per buyer, rate limiting on the purchase endpoint, and refunds of voided cards default to the original method.
Staff void a card and pocket the refundVoid needs its own permission. Every void and refund records who did it, the destination and the reason in the payment audit log.
Wrong recipient number typedStaff can correct the number while the card is unclaimed. After that, void and reissue.
One business reading another's walletsEvery query carries business_id. A wallet at one business is invisible to another.
Rounding driftAmounts are DECIMAL(10,2) and handled as whole piastres in code, never as floating point.
Phone numbers in logsThe existing masking helpers are used on every new log line.
Expiry on the wrong dayExpiry is stored as an instant computed from the business's timezone. Earlier UTC assumptions in this codebase shifted class times by hours, so this gets its own tests.

Open questions

What this plan assumed. Each line says what the demo does today, so a "yes" means no change.

  1. Do gift cards expire once they are in the wallet?Story #1090 says gift cards do not expire. Your answer says wallet credit expires after the owner's period. Assumed: a waiting card never expires, and the clock starts the day it is added to a wallet.
  2. Should refunded money expire at all?A refund sent to the wallet is the client's own payment. Letting it lapse after 6 months may conflict with consumer protection rules in Egypt. Assumed: it expires like any credit, and the client is told the date. Worth a legal check before launch. A separate, longer period for refund credit is an easy alternative.
  3. Does the popup also appear for booking refunds, or only for gift cards?Assumed: every refund staff start by hand shows it, with the Wallet configuration setting preselecting the answer. Client cancellations follow the setting silently.
  4. Who gets the refund when a card is voided?Assumed: the sender, and only the unused amount. The recipient loses the remaining credit and is told.
  5. Is the recipient's email still required when a client buys?Matching is by phone only. Assumed: required in the client app as #1090 says, optional at the front desk, and used only for the notification.
  6. Does the client see a voided card?Left open in #1100. Assumed: yes, in history, marked voided, so the missing credit is explained.
  7. What do "Story 2.1" and "Story 2.3 of the Osana PRD" say?Three stories depend on them for admin payment recording and the wallet balance definition. This plan used the existing front desk checkout and the existing ledger. The document is needed to confirm.
  8. How are gift cards counted in revenue?Selling a card and later spending it must not count twice in reports or in the Odoo sync. Assumed: the sale is the revenue event and wallet payments are reported as a payment method, not new income. Finance should confirm.
  9. Limits on amounts.The demo uses a minimum of EGP 50 and presets of 250, 500, 1,000 and 2,000. Minimum, maximum and presets need a decision, per business or global.
  10. Does the front-page chip show the whole wallet?#1094 says gift card balance. Since gift cards now merge into the wallet, assumed: the chip shows the full wallet balance.
  11. Money returned to a gift card near its expiry.Assumed: it keeps the card's original date, but never less than 30 days from the refund.
  12. Does cashback keep its own expiry?Campaigns already set cashback expiry in days. Assumed: campaign credit keeps the campaign's value and the owner's period covers everything else.
  13. A company needs a phone number.A company is a client record and client phone numbers are unique. Assumed: the company's contact number is used.
